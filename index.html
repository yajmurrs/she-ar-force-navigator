Mission notes

Read before you quote us

What this is

  • The directions to the Sun and Earth come from NASA/JPL Horizons. They are sampled once an hour, from 14 Nov to 14 Dec 2026 (UTC).
  • The terrain skyline comes from a LOLA elevation model: 20 m resolution in the near field and 80 m in the far field. The first 40 m around the observer are left out.
  • A body counts as visible when the center of its disc is above that skyline.
  • A teammate prepared the hourly records, and this page reads them as supplied.

What it is not

  • This is not a prediction of continuous sunlight, power, a radio link or a safe landing. It is not a NASA product.
  • The Sun and Earth are treated as points. Disc size, eclipses and link budgets are not modeled.
  • The three places are research samples. In the source data, region boundaries and landing safety are marked as unverified.
  • NR-03 shows Earth in 0 of 721 hours. The skyline toward Earth sits between 16.6° and 17.7°, while Earth peaks at only 9.7°. This result needs independent verification.
  • On screen, the skyline is rebuilt from the samples along the Sun and Earth tracks, and the gaps are interpolated. Elevation is exaggerated, and the nearer foreground ridges are illustrative only.
  • Nearest-pixel and bilinear terrain sampling produce different results.
